* Expect to be asked why these differ from the published figures. The brief quotes 1.2 M / 3.5 M / 11.2 M parameters; we measure 0.73 M / 2.23 M / 11.18 M. The gap is the classifier head. SqueezeNet's ImageNet head is a 512→1000 conv (~0.51 M parameters) and MobileNetV2's is a 1280→1000 linear (~1.28 M); swapping both for 5-class heads removes almost exactly the difference. ResNet-18 barely moves because its 512→1000 head is small relative to 11 M. This is a measurement, not a discrepancy — and being able to explain it is worth more than matching the slide.

Demo Script — eight beats, about five minutes

  1. Open · 20s
    Start on MobileNetV2, clean air. Point at ρ = 0.288, an empty buffer, a dominant diagonal. "This is the system working. Hold that picture."
  2. The trap · 40s
    Press 3 for ResNet-18. It is the most accurate model available. Let it run.
  3. The failure · 50s
    ρ climbs to 1.100, the buffer fills, the warning tone fires at 8/10, frames start dropping, the health bar bleeds out. Say the line: a 97.6% model that drops a tenth of its input is not a 97.6% system.
  4. Theory check · 30s
    Point at the Pollaczek–Khinchine panel beside the live gauge. The closed form predicted this before the simulation ran it.
  5. Overcorrect · 40s
    Press 1 for SqueezeNet. Drops stop instantly — ρ = 0.113. Looks fixed.
  6. The second failure · 50s
    Trigger heat haze at full intensity. Watch the BEND row lose its diagonal and the false-negative rate climb. Nothing is being dropped and racks are still going uninspected — a different mechanism, the same outcome.
  7. The trade · 40s
    Deploy the bilateral kernel. Accuracy recovers; the service bar grows; headroom shrinks. Add all three kernels and the budget goes negative. Robustness is bought with latency out of the same 40 ms.
  8. Land it · 30s
    Press 2. MobileNetV2 at 11.5 ms leaves 28.5 ms of headroom — enough to run a kernel when a storm hits and still make the deadline. That headroom is the engineering answer.

Viva — questions and answers

Why is MobileNetV2 optimal here?
Inverted residual blocks with linear bottlenecks decouple spatial filtering (a depthwise 3×3) from feature mixing (pointwise 1×1 convolutions). That cuts compute from 1.8 GFLOPs to roughly 300 MFLOPs while keeping enough representational capacity to avoid SqueezeNet's false-negative rate under distribution shift. At 11.5 ms it leaves 28.5 ms of headroom in a 40 ms budget — room to run a preprocessing kernel and still make the deadline.
What does the M/D/1 queue demonstrate?
That a model cannot be evaluated in isolation. ResNet-18 is 97.6% accurate and is a system-level failure: at 44 ms it serves 22.7 fps against a 25 fps camera, so ρ = 1.10, the backlog grows without bound, and the buffer discards frames. Accuracy measured only on the frames a model actually sees is a survivorship statistic.
How does augmentation improve robustness?
Under heat haze or strobe glare the baseline misreads fiber bends as nominal racks — visible as the BEND row's mass sliding into column 0. Deploying edge preprocessing restores diagonal dominance on the live matrix. But each kernel bills to the same service-time budget as inference, so there is no free robustness — the panel shows headroom going negative when you stack all three.
Why Poisson arrivals if the camera is a fixed 25 FPS?
Jitter in capture, encode and transport makes inter-arrival times effectively memoryless by the time frames reach the compute node. It is also the pessimistic assumption, and buffers should be sized against the pessimistic case. Perfectly periodic arrivals into a deterministic server would queue nothing at all until ρ hit exactly 1.
Why is L_q for M/D/1 half the M/M/1 value?
Pollaczek–Khinchine gives L_q = λ²·E[S²] / (2(1−ρ)) for M/G/1. With deterministic service E[S²] = E[S]², which collapses to ρ²/(2(1−ρ)) — exactly half the M/M/1 result. Removing variance from service time halves your buffering requirement at identical load, for free.
Why does SqueezeNet beat MobileNetV2 on the clock with more MACs?
Arithmetic intensity. Dense 3×3 convolutions saturate the vector units and reuse operands from cache; depthwise convolutions do one multiply per loaded weight, so they are memory-bandwidth bound and achieve poor utilisation. MACs predict energy; they do not predict latency. The cockpit shows MACs-retired-per-millisecond for exactly this.
Which parts are real and which are simulated?
Real: parameter counts, MAC counts, tensor shapes, inference latency (perf_counter around a genuine forward pass), and preprocessing kernel cost. Modelled: which class is predicted and with what confidence, because the backbones are not fine-tuned on labelled rack photography — no such dataset exists. Latency is measured; the label comes from a documented capacity-limited confusion kernel. Both are stated in the UI.
Why is the most accurate model the worst calibrated?
Overconfidence grows with capacity (Guo et al., 2017) — ResNet-18 carries the highest ECE in the zoo. It matters operationally: if you wanted to route only low-confidence frames to the heavy model, you would need confidences you can trust. An overconfident model never asks for help.
Why macro-F1 and not accuracy?
The stream is 55% nominal, so accuracy is dominated by the easy majority class and reads ~0.9 even for a model that catches no thermal hotspots at all. Macro-F1 weights the rare critical classes equally. Reporting accuracy on an imbalanced safety task is how you ship a detector that detects nothing.
